# Weak outlook: bid versus estimate

Not a second SKILL. Live rules in `SKILL.md` and `references/prognosis.md` win. There is **no** sell-price tool.

## When

Speak this only on **weak outlook** (`poor`): the job may be large, and a repair often leaves a car that is hard to sell. Spoken line: **Repair may cost more than the car.**

Use it after the fault statement, in **[Sell]**, with **[Repair]** first (verified headline, or we publish no figure). Default `poor` paths include oil-pressure, red coolant, hydraulic brake Stop, flashing engine, ICE charging plus belt / heavy steering / rising temperature, DPF flash / limp / oil over maximum, and AdBlue remaining-starts / no-start. Upgrade a garage bucket only if they already have a large written estimate, or the lamp card says so.

Do **not** push selling on close-it-yourself or in-range garage. Skip when Step 5 already ended as not-a-fault.

A missing used-car pound is not a missing outlook. Still give the decision rule.

Spoken **[Outlook]** stays in the live ~40–60 word envelope. Compress this copy; do not drop the rule.

## Owner copy

We publish no used-car price. Get one instant-sale or dealer bid as the car sits: as a runner if you may still drive it, or not a runner if you must not drive it in. Get one written garage estimate. Put the estimate next to the bid before you authorise the job. If the estimate is larger than the bid, selling is often the better outlook. If you must not drive it in, recovery or collection is part of the sell cost; we publish no fee for that either. The repair may cost more than a buyer will pay for it as it sits.

## Red lines

- Do not invent Parkers, WeBuyAnyCar, Autotrader, or “typical trade-in” pounds. No sell-price API means no figure.
- Do not invent a recovery or collection fee.
- Do not say the car is a write-off. Say the repair may cost more than a buyer will pay for it as it sits.
- Do not fill a missing bid or estimate with a model guess.
- One bid as it sits, one written estimate for the compare. Do not skip **[Sell]** because pounds are missing.
- Do not name the failed part. A cost slug is not a diagnosis.
- Do not put a shop or scanner link above Stop / recovery.
- Do not push a sell path when outlook is close-it-yourself or garage in range.

## Pass / fail

- Pass: “We publish no used-car price. Get one bid as it sits and one written estimate.”
- Fail: “Parkers has it at about £2,400” / “WeBuyAnyCar will give you around £800.”
- Pass: runner bid if they may still drive it; not-a-runner / as-it-sits bid if they must not.
- Fail: a runner price on a Stop car, or telling them to drive it in for a better bid.
- Pass: “If the estimate is larger than the bid, selling is often the better outlook.”
- Fail: “It’s a write-off.”
- Pass: “Recovery is part of the sell cost” when **[Drive advice]** is Stop, with no invented fee.
- Fail: omitting recovery on Stop, or “recovery is usually £150.”
- Pass: restating **[Drive advice]** and **[Outlook]** if they ask repair versus sell.
- Fail: inventing a trade-in, diagnosing the part, or dropping **[Sell]** because there is no price tool.
