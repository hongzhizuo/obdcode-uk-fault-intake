# Ordinary brakes still work; ABS does not

Not a second SKILL. Live rules: `SKILL.md`, `references/prognosis.md`, `references/prognosis-cards.md`, lamp 11 in `references/warning-lights.md`. If they disagree, those win. Full ABS card: `11-abs.md`. This note is only the **working-brakes** split: Limited driving, garage, not a DIY close.

Lamp **11** `abs`. Amber `ABS` in a circle with brackets. `safety_class: Amber`. `drive_advice: Limited`. `warnings: W-SUPPORT`. Bucket **`garage`**.

---

## When

Run this after the Step 5 statement when they picked **11**, or named ABS / anti-lock, and they have **not** reported a spongy pedal, a pull, a leak, or a red brake lamp that stays on with the parking brake / EPB / Auto Hold fully off.

That mix is lamp **3** `brake-system` (`poor`, Stop). Do not keep “drive it in” speech on hydraulic facts.

Skip outlook on a key-on bulb check that then went out.

Do not upgrade to `poor` because the car is old. Upgrade only if they already have a **large hydraulic quote**. Vans: same bucket. Say “your Transit”. A day off the road may be speech. Do not invent a day-rate.

## Owner copy

Spoken **[Outlook]** after the statement. ≤120 words. No ids, no fusion slugs, no URLs, no plates, no pounds.

A garage can usually handle this. Ordinary brakes still work; anti-lock does not. That is why you may drive directly to a workshop — no extra journeys. Leave extra stopping distance. Avoid heavy braking on wet, loose, or icy ground. We publish no figure for this class of job. Ask two workshops for a written estimate. A scan is the next step; the lamp does not name the part. Working ordinary brakes is drive advice, not a job you close on the driveway. Do not bleed, lift, or clear the lamp. If the red brake lamp stays on with the parking brake fully off, or the pedal is spongy, do not drive it in — arrange recovery.

## What “still works” means

| Still works | Does not work |
|---|---|
| Ordinary (foundation) brakes | Anti-lock |
| Pedal still stops the car in normal use | Hard braking on wet, loose, or ice: wheels can lock and the car slides instead of steering |
| They may drive **directly** to a garage | Extra journeys, motorway loops, “finish the week” |

On many cars an ABS fault also drops stability control and hill-hold. Say that as **drive advice**, not as a named failed part.

**Limited** (SKILL Step 5): they may drive directly there, no extra journeys. It is **not** YesWithCare. It is **not** Stop unless hydraulic facts take it to lamp 3.

Working ordinary brakes is **[Drive advice]**. It is not a repair, not a close, and not permission to ignore the lamp.

## Garage, not DIY

**Bucket:** `garage`. Owner hears: a garage can usually handle this.

**Repair:** no ABS slug. Do not call `repair_cost`. Empty allowlist means **we publish no figure** — two written estimates.

Do **not** call `car-diagnostic-test-cost` (not on this allowlist; a scan at the garage is still the next step in speech), `brake-pads-and-discs-cost` as the cause of this lamp, or `wheel-bearing-replacement-cost` unless **they already named that job**. Then speak it only as **if later invoiced**. A cost page is not today’s failed part.

**Sell:** not first-line. Do not push selling on garage. Sell talk only if they already have a large hydraulic quote (`poor`): one bid as it sits, one written estimate, no invented pounds. ABS-only Limited driving is still a runner unless hydraulics took it to Stop.

**Close it yourself: no.**

- No hydraulics. No bleeding. No ABS bleed. No lifting. No pads. No driveway module or sensor work.
- A reader does not close this. Do not clear the lamp. Do not describe scan-tool bleed or coding as owner work.
- Owner-safe notes for the garage only: whether it came on with the red brake lamp or the skid-lines lamp; whether it comes and goes at a particular speed. Information, not a repair.
- Traction-button check belongs on lamp **12**, not as an ABS close.

This path has no `[Close it]` block. Device and shop links stay below Stop / recovery.

**Ask the garage:** which wheel the scan reports. Do not offer sensor / reluctor / wiring as a closed list.

MOT: an ABS malfunction lamp can be a listed Major where ABS is required. Link the [DVSA inspection manual](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/mot-inspection-manual-for-private-passenger-and-light-commercial-vehicles). Never say “Expect a fail.” Same-system History (ABS, wheel speed sensor) ends: **this does not show the cause of today’s lamp.**

## Red lines

1. Do not diagnose. Not the sensor, reluctor, wiring, module, pump, or bearing — even as a shortlist.
2. Do not treat working ordinary brakes as a DIY close, a reason to ignore the lamp, or permission for extra journeys.
3. Do not invent pounds for repair, sell, or “typical ABS jobs.”
4. Do not call a nearby slug because it has a number.
5. Do not mix this card with lamp 3 Stop speech unless they already reported hydraulic facts.
6. Do not give hydraulic or lift how-to. No ABS bleed on the driveway.
7. Do not clear the lamp as a fix.
8. Do not say “Expect a fail.”
9. Do not print, file, or speak a real registration. “Your 2016 Fiesta” / “your Transit” only.
10. A cost slug is not a diagnosis.

## Pass / fail

**Pass**

- “Ordinary brakes still work; anti-lock does not.”
- “A garage can usually handle this.”
- Limited: drive gently and **directly** there; extra stopping distance; avoid heavy braking on wet or loose ground; no extra journeys.
- “Working ordinary brakes is drive advice, not a driveway close.”
- “We publish no figure — two written estimates.”
- “A scan is the next step; the lamp does not name the part.”
- Hydraulic facts (red brake lamp with parking brake off, spongy pedal, pull, leak) → Stop; do not drive it in; recover.
- Restating Limited / recovery when they ask keep-driving or repair-vs-sell.
- “This skill does not diagnose” when they ask which part.

**Fail**

- “Normal brakes still work, so you can ignore it.”
- “Normal brakes still work, so finish this on the driveway.”
- “Bleed it yourself” / “clear the code and see” / any lift or hydraulic DIY.
- “Drive as usual” / extra journeys / “pop out later.”
- “It’s the wheel-speed sensor / reluctor / wiring / ABS pump.”
- “Sensor, reluctor, or wiring.”
- “About £300–£800” with no tool result, or any guessed ABS price.
- Calling wheel-bearing or pads-and-discs because the ABS lamp is on.
- “Expect a fail.”
- Invented trade-in or “worth about …” pounds.
- Naming a likely part when they ask what is wrong.
