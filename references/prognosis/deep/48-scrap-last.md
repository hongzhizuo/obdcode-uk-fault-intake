# Scrap is last

Not a second SKILL. Live rules: `SKILL.md`, `references/prognosis.md` (Sell; Weak outlook without a sell number), `references/prognosis-cards.md`. If this file disagrees, those win. Do not diagnose. Do not invent pounds. Do not add a 14th lamp.

Scrap is **not** the opening line on a weak outlook. It is the **last** money path, and only when the car is **not a runner** and a **written repair estimate already exceeds any bid they actually received**.

## When

Run this speech only on **weak outlook** (`poor`), after **[Drive advice]** and the usual repair-or-sell lines.

Typical **not a runner** facts: **[Drive advice]** is Stop. Do not drive it in. Oil-pressure, red coolant, flashing engine, hydraulic brake (parking brake off, or spongy pedal / pull / leak), charging plus belt / heavy steering / rising temp on ICE, power steering already heavy, AdBlue remaining-starts / will not start.

Do **not** mention scrap on:

- Close it yourself (`owner` / `device`)
- Garage, cost in range — do not push selling, and do not jump to scrap
- Not-a-fault skips (bulb check, blue thermometer that went out, parking brake still on with a normal pedal, ESC flashing while driving)
- Age or MOT mileage alone. Do not upgrade `garage` to `poor` because the car is old unless they already have a large estimate, or the lamp’s card is already weak.

Vans: same order. Say **your Transit**. Downtime is “a day off the road,” not a made-up day-rate.

## Decision order

There is **no** sell-price tool and **no** scrap-price tool. A missing pound is not a missing outlook. Give the rule.

1. **Repair.** Verified `repair_cost` headline for an allowlisted slug, or: we publish no figure — two written local estimates. Do not hunt a nearby slug. A cost page is not a diagnosis.
2. **Sell as it sits.** Get **one** instant-sale or dealer bid **as the car sits** — for parts if it must not be driven. Get **one** written garage estimate.
3. **Compare.** If the estimate is larger than that bid, **selling is often the better outlook**. That is still a **sell** (runner if they may drive it; parts if they must not). It is not scrap, and it is not a write-off.
4. **Scrap last.** Only after a not-a-runner car has a written estimate that exceeds **any** bid they actually got — including the as-it-sits / parts bid. Do not open with “just scrap it.” Do not skip the bid.

Do not say the car is a write-off. Say the repair may cost more than a buyer will pay for it as it sits, so they should put the estimate next to a bid before they authorise the job.

If they ask repair vs sell vs scrap: restate **[Drive advice]** and **[Outlook]**. This skill does not diagnose.

## Collection

When **[Drive advice]** is Stop, recovery / collection is part of the **sell** cost, and of any later scrap path. Ask the buyer or the yard to **collect**, or call recovery. Do not tell them to drive it to a buyer or a scrapyard.

Do not invent that fee. “Recovery is about £150” is a fail, same as an invented scrap cheque.

On Limited / drive-with-care, the car may still be a runner. Collection is not automatic. Scrap talk still does not belong on those buckets unless they already have a large estimate and a weak outlook.

## No invented scrap pounds

Do not invent:

- Scrap value (“typical scrap is £200–£400”, “the metal is worth about…”)
- Parkers / WeBuyAnyCar / “typical trade-in”
- Collection or recovery fees
- A write-off or salvage category in pounds

If they already have a **written** scrap, collection, or instant-sale figure, you may repeat **that** number as theirs. Do not mint a typical scrap cheque. Do not print a plate. Call it **your 2016 Fiesta**.

## Owner copy

≤120 words. After the statement. Stop / recovery already sits in **[Drive advice]** — no shop link above it. No ids, no fusion slugs, no plates, no guessed GBP.

Repair may cost more than a buyer will pay as it sits. We publish no used-car price and no scrap figure. Get one written estimate and one bid as it sits — it is not a runner, so for parts, with collection. If that estimate is larger than any bid, selling as it sits is often better than repairing. Scrap is last. Do not drive it in.

(69 words.)

Do not add a parts guess. Do not name the failed part.

## Red lines

1. Do not lead with scrap. Do not skip the as-it-sits bid.
2. Do not say write-off, total loss, or “it’s only scrap now” as the first outlook.
3. No invented scrap, sell, or collection pounds.
4. Do not tell a Stop car to drive to a scrapyard or an instant-sale branch.
5. Do not push scrap on garage or close-it-yourself.
6. Do not diagnose. Do not treat scrap as proof of a named part.
7. No real registration in this file or in speech.
8. Safety before commerce. No scanner, shop, or parts link above Stop / recovery.
