# Do not say write-off

Not a second SKILL. Live rules: `SKILL.md` Step 6, `references/prognosis.md` (Weak outlook without a sell number; spoken **[Outlook]**). If this file disagrees, those win. Do not diagnose. Do not invent pounds. Do not add a 14th lamp id.

Do **not** tell the owner the car is a write-off. Say the repair may cost more than a buyer will pay for it as it sits, so they should put an estimate next to a bid before they authorise the job.

Spoken bucket line: **Repair may cost more than the car.**

## When

Use this wording on **weak outlook** (`poor`) — after the fault statement, in **[Outlook]** / **[Sell]**.

Default `poor` paths include oil-pressure, red coolant, hydraulic brake Stop, flashing engine, ICE charging plus belt / heavy steering / rising temperature, DPF flash / limp / oil over maximum, and AdBlue remaining-starts / no-start. Upgrade a garage card to this speech only if they already have a large written estimate, or that lamp’s card says so.

Also use it when they ask “is it a write-off?”, “total loss?”, or “should I scrap it?” Restate **[Drive advice]** and **[Outlook]**. Do not adopt their word as a verdict.

Do **not** use write-off (or this sell compare) on close-it-yourself or in-range garage. Age, high miles, or MOT history alone do not make a garage car a write-off.

Skip when Step 5 already ended as not-a-fault.

## Why the word is banned

A **write-off** in UK speech is an insurer or salvage verdict (often CAT A / B / S / N). This skill does not insure the car, does not inspect it, and does not publish a used-car price. Saying “it’s a write-off” is a diagnosis of value we cannot make.

A missing sell pound is not a missing outlook. Still give the **decision rule**.

## Owner copy

≤120 words. Spoken after the statement. UK English. No ids, fusion slugs, plates, or guessed GBP.

Repair may cost more than a buyer will pay for it as it sits. We publish no used-car price. Get one bid as it sits and one written garage estimate. Put the estimate next to the bid before you authorise the job. If the estimate is larger than the bid, selling is often the better outlook.

(62 words. Compress into the live ~40–60 **[Outlook]** envelope; do not drop the rule.)

Stop lamps: it is usually not a runner. Recovery or collection is part of the sell cost. Do not invent that fee. Do not tell them to drive it to a buyer.

If they already hold a **written** insurer letter or salvage category, you may repeat **that** document as theirs. Do not mint a CAT code, and do not turn our outlook into “so it is a write-off.”

## What to say instead

| Banned | Say |
|---|---|
| “It’s a write-off.” / “written off” / “write it off” | “Repair may cost more than the car.” |
| “Total loss.” / “CAT S / N / A / B.” as our verdict | “The repair may cost more than a buyer will pay as it sits.” |
| “Just scrap it” as the first line | Bid as it sits versus a written estimate. Scrap is last, and only on a not-a-runner path after that compare (`48-scrap-last.md`) |
| “Typical trade-in is £…” | “We publish no used-car price. Get one bid as it sits.” |

## Red lines

1. Never say write-off, written off, write it off, total loss, or an insurer CAT code as **this skill’s** verdict.
2. Never invent Parkers, WeBuyAnyCar, trade-in, scrap, or recovery pounds.
3. Never skip **[Sell]** on weak outlook because there is no price tool.
4. Never push this compare on close-it-yourself or garage-in-range.
5. Never upgrade `garage` to `poor` because the car is old, unless they already have a large estimate or the card is already `poor`.
6. Never name the failed part. A cost slug is not a diagnosis.
7. Never put a shop or scanner link above Stop / recovery.
8. Never print a real plate. Say **your 2016 Fiesta** or **your Transit**.

## Pass versus fail

- Pass: “Repair may cost more than the car.”
- Fail: “It’s a write-off.”
- Pass: “The repair may cost more than a buyer will pay for it as it sits. Put an estimate next to a bid before you authorise the job.”
- Fail: “Written off — only scrap.” / “Total loss.” / “That’s CAT S.”
- Pass: “We publish no used-car price. Get one bid as it sits and one written estimate.”
- Fail: “Parkers has it at £800, so write it off.”
- Pass: If they ask “is it a write-off?” — restate Stop or Limited, then the bid-versus-estimate rule. Do not answer yes.
- Fail: “Yes, it’s a write-off.”
- Pass: garage or close-it-yourself — no sell push, no write-off talk.
- Fail: “It’s old, write it off” on a TPMS inflate, a handbook DPF regen, or a routine garage lamp.
- Pass: they already have an insurer letter — repeat that it is theirs; still no CAT code from us.
- Fail: inventing a salvage category so the sentence can avoid the word write-off.
