# 11 `abs`

Live defaults: `references/prognosis.md`, `references/prognosis-cards.md`. This file is not a second SKILL. If they disagree, those win.

Lamp 11. Amber `ABS` in a circle with brackets. Bucket **`garage`**. Not diagnosis. Not a driveway close.

---

## When

Run Step 6 after the Step 5 statement when this lamp is a **real fault**: it stayed on after the key-on bulb check.

Skip outlook when it was only the bulb check and then went out.

Stay on this card when they picked 11, or named ABS / anti-lock, and they have **not** reported a spongy pedal, a pull, a leak, or a red brake lamp that stays on with the parking brake / EPB / Auto Hold fully off. Those hydraulic facts are lamp 3 `brake-system` (`poor`, Stop). Do not keep ABS-only “drive it in” speech on that mix.

Default remains **`garage`**. Do not upgrade to `poor` because the car is old. Upgrade only if they already have a **large hydraulic quote**.

Vans: same bucket. Say “your Transit”. A day off the road may be part of outlook speech. Do not invent a day-rate.

History (statement, not outlook): same-system MOT notes only — ABS, wheel speed sensor. Quote date and type. No causal verbs. End: **this does not show the cause of today’s lamp.**

MOT talk is gated. An ABS malfunction lamp can be a listed Major where ABS is required. Link the [DVSA inspection manual](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/mot-inspection-manual-for-private-passenger-and-light-commercial-vehicles). Never say “Expect a fail.”

## Owner copy

Spoken outlook, ≤120 words. After the statement. No ids, no fusion slugs, no URLs, no plates, no pounds.

A garage can usually handle this. We publish no figure for this class of job. Ask two workshops for a written estimate. A scan is the next step; the lamp does not name the part. Ordinary brakes still work; anti-lock does not. Drive gently and directly there. Leave extra stopping distance. Avoid heavy braking on wet or loose ground. That is drive advice, not a job you close on the driveway. Do not bleed, lift, or clear the lamp. If the red brake lamp stays on with the parking brake fully off, or the pedal is spongy, do not drive it in — arrange recovery.

(104 words.)

If they already have a large hydraulic quote, swap the first line for weak-outlook speech: repair may cost more than a buyer will pay as it sits. Still no invented bid.

## Sell

Not first-line. Garage bucket: do not push selling.

Sell talk only on the `poor` branch — they already have a large hydraulic quote:

1. Repair: no published figure unless a named slug above actually returned a headline.
2. Sell: we publish no used-car price. Get **one** bid as it sits and **one** written estimate. Compare before they authorise the job.
3. Recovery / collection is part of sell cost only when **[Drive advice]** is Stop. ABS-only Limited driving is still a runner unless hydraulics took it to Stop.
4. Do not say write-off. Do not invent Parkers / trade-in / collection pounds.

A lamp-on ABS car is harder to present as a safe runner. That is speech, not a number.

## DIY

**No.** Close-it-yourself is not allowed on this lamp.

- No hydraulics. No bleeding. No ABS bleed. No lifting. No pads. No driveway module or sensor work.
- Ordinary brakes still working is **[Drive advice]** (Limited: drive directly there, no extra journeys). It is not a fix and not a close.
- A reader does not close this. Do not clear the lamp. Do not describe scan-tool bleed or coding as owner work.
- Owner-safe notes for the garage only: whether it came on with the red brake lamp or the skid-lines lamp; whether it comes and goes at a particular speed. That is information, not a repair.
- If ESC / traction is also steady, still garage. Do not name a shared part. Traction-button check belongs on lamp 12, not as an ABS close.

Device and shop links stay below Stop / recovery. This path has no `[Close it]` block.

## Red lines

1. Do not diagnose. Not the sensor, reluctor, wiring, module, pump, or bearing — even as a shortlist.
2. Do not invent pounds for repair, sell, or “typical ABS jobs.”
3. Do not print, file, or speak a real registration. Illustrative plate only: `AB12CDE`. After lookup: “your 2016 Fiesta”, never the plate.
4. Do not call `wheel-bearing-replacement-cost` because this lamp is on.
5. Do not treat working ordinary brakes as permission to ignore it, keep extra journeys, or bleed it at home.
6. Do not give hydraulic or lift how-to. Red-class brake work stays at the workshop.
7. Do not clear the lamp as a fix.
8. Do not say “Expect a fail.”
9. Do not mix this card with lamp 3 Stop speech unless they already reported hydraulic facts.
10. A cost slug is not a diagnosis.

## Pass / fail

**Pass**

- “A garage can usually handle this.”
- “A scan is the next step; the lamp does not name the part.”
- “We publish no figure — two written estimates.”
- “Ordinary brakes still work; anti-lock does not. Drive gently and directly there.” (drive advice)
- “If the garage later invoices a wheel bearing, published UK figures are …” — only after they named that job, and only after `repair_cost`.
- Restating Limited / recovery from the statement when they ask keep-driving or repair-vs-sell.
- “This skill does not diagnose” when they ask which part.

**Fail**

- “It’s the wheel-speed sensor / reluctor / wiring / ABS pump.”
- “Sensor, reluctor, or wiring.”
- “About £300–£800” with no tool result, or any guessed ABS price.
- Calling `wheel-bearing-replacement-cost` or pads-and-discs because the ABS lamp is on.
- “Bleed it yourself” / “clear the code and see” / any lift or hydraulic DIY.
- “Normal brakes still work, so you can finish this on the driveway” or “so you can ignore it.”
- “Expect a fail.”
- Invented trade-in or “worth about …” pounds.
- Naming a likely part when they ask what is wrong.
