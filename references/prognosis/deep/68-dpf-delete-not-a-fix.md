# DPF delete is not a cheap fix

Not a second SKILL. Live rules: `SKILL.md`, `references/prognosis.md`, `references/prognosis-cards.md`, `references/value-gain.md`. If this file disagrees with those four, those four win.

Never suggest cutting, blanking, or deleting a factory diesel particulate filter as a cheap DPF repair. The sale-price band is **Negative**. It is illegal on a used-on-road car. No how-to.

Full diesel lamp 9 outlook stays on `09-dpf.md`. Path B “will a delete raise value?” for delete / remap / illegal exhaust stays on `71-illegal-mods-negative.md`. This file is the **repair refusal**: delete is not the cheap job.

## When

Use this note when they treat removal as the inexpensive DPF fix, including:

- Diesel lamp **9** (or they named the exhaust box with dots) and they ask to delete, cut, blank, remap-out, or “just take the filter out”
- A garage has quoted a clean or a filter job and they ask whether delete is cheaper
- “Just cut it out” / “cheaper than a DPF”
- “Will deleting it fix the lamp / pass the test for less?”

Lookup the plate if they gave one, for year / make / model. **No** real plate in speech or in git.

Then pick the path:

| Facts | What runs first | This file adds |
|---|---|---|
| Diesel DPF lamp, or a DPF bill, **and** they want a delete | Path A: statement + lamp outlook (`09-dpf.md`) | Then a separate **[Value] Negative**. Do not change the bucket to “close it by cutting it out.” |
| No lamp — they only ask if delete is a cheap repair or adds value | Path B. **No** dashboard picker | **[Value] Negative.** Illegal on a road car. No how-to. |

Petrol / hybrid GPF or cat delete as a “cheap fix” is the same refusal (band **Negative**, no how-to). Do **not** run diesel handbook-regen copy. Unmatched GPF outlook is `14-gpf-unmatched.md`.

AdBlue / urea / DEF is not a DPF delete question. Do not map it to 9.

## Owner copy

≤120 words. Price effect and the repair refusal only. No slugs, no URLs, no plates. No pounds unless they already stated an invoice (repeat as **spend**) or the live `repair_cost` tool returned a cleaning headline in this chat.

### Lamp or DPF bill, and they want a delete (~100 words)

Do not cut the filter out. That is not a DPF repair. Removing a factory DPF is illegal on a used-on-road car. Buyers, testers, and insurers treat it as a defect. The sale band is Negative — the bid shrinks. We publish no gain in pounds. Steady lamp, driving normally: one handbook regen outdoors, then a garage if it stays. Cleaning figure only if the cost tool returns one; no published replacement-filter price. Flash, limp, or oil over maximum: recovery; bid as it sits versus a written estimate. Do not instruct a delete.

Spoken labels (after **[Drive advice]** and **[Outlook]** from the DPF card):

```
[Value] Negative. Deletion is not a cheap DPF repair. Illegal on a used-on-road car. Buyers, testers, and insurers treat a missing factory filter as a defect. We publish no gain in pounds. Do not do this to a road car.
```

Do not fold delete into **[Close it]** or **[Repair]**.

### No lamp — “is delete cheaper / worth more?” (~57 words)

```
[Value] Negative. Deleting the DPF is not a cheap repair and it does not raise the sale price. It is illegal on a used-on-road car. Buyers, testers, and insurers treat it as a defect, not an upgrade. We publish no gain in pounds. Do not do this to a road car.
```

No **[Record]** as a selling point. The vehicle card is for service, repair, presentation, and **legal** modification. Do not present a delete as a premium.

If that work is already on the car, the band is still **Negative**. Garage restoration of a factory filter is not this card and is not a how-to.

## What to say instead of a delete

Diesel DPF path only — copy from `09-dpf.md` / `prognosis-cards.md`, then this **[Value]**:

| Facts | Bucket | Repair speech | Delete? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steady amber, driving normally | `owner`, then `garage` if **one** handbook regen does not clear it | Call `dpf-cleaning-cost`. Headline or: we publish no figure — two written estimates | No. Never “just cut it out.” |
| Flash, limp, red warning, or oil over maximum | `poor` | Same slug. No published DPF-replace price. Do not invent one | No. Missing factory DPF is a test and value problem |

Sell talk only on the **poor** branch (or a later large written estimate): one bid **as it sits**, one written estimate, recovery in the sell cost if they must not drive it in. A delete does not make it a legal runner.

Vans: same refusal. Say **your Transit**. Downtime is “a day off the road,” not a made-up day-rate, and not a reason to delete.

## Band

**Negative.** Shrinks the buyer pool or the bid. Illegal work is also a legal / test / insurer problem, not a valuation uplift.

| Work | Band | Skill line |
|---|---|---|
| DPF / GPF / cat removal | Negative | Illegal on a used-on-road car. Buyers, testers, and insurers treat it as a defect, not an upgrade |

If they already have a written invoice for a delete or a remap, you may repeat **that** figure as what they paid, then this band. Spend is not uplift. Paying for a delete does not raise the sale price.

`repair_cost` is never a **gain**. `dpf-cleaning-cost` is the published **job** on the diesel DPF path, often `gbp: null`. There is no delete slug. Do not invent “delete is about £X so it is cheaper.”

## Red lines

1. **No delete as a repair.** Not cheap, not a close-it-yourself, not “tell the garage to cut it out.”
2. **No how-to:** no cut, blank, weld, remap-out, off-map, or “put it back” steps.
3. **Negative** on a used-on-road car. Do not call it an upgrade. Do not instruct.
4. Not legal advice beyond “do not do this to a road car.” No invented fines, points, or jail terms.
5. No invented pounds for a delete, a filter, a gain, or a used-car bid.
6. Do not change a DPF bucket to `owner` / `device` because they asked for a delete.
7. No scan-tool forced regen and no lamp-clear as a substitute fix (`26-never-clear-never-forced-regen.md`).
8. No diesel regen copy on petrol / hybrid GPF. No AdBlue mapped to 9.
9. No diagnosis: not “blocked DPF,” not soot-loaded versus ash-loaded.
10. MOT: testers may treat a missing factory filter as a defect. Do not say “Expect a fail.” Gate on first-use + fuel; link the [DVSA inspection manual](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/mot-inspection-manual-for-private-passenger-and-light-commercial-vehicles).
11. Safety / legality before any buyer-taste talk. Do not put this **[Value]** above a Stop / recovery line.
12. No real plate. No SAE J2012 wording. Do not add a 14th lamp id.

## Pass / fail

**Pass**

- “Deletion is not a DPF repair.” / “Do not cut it out.”
- **[Value] Negative.** Illegal on a used-on-road car. Buyers, testers, and insurers treat it as a defect.
- “We publish no gain in pounds.” “Do not do this to a road car.”
- Lamp + delete question: **[Drive advice]** and DPF **[Outlook]** first, then a **separate** **[Value]**.
- Steady / driving normally: one handbook regen, then garage — not a delete.
- Flash / limp / oil over max: `poor`; recovery; bid as it sits versus a written estimate — still no delete.
- Call `dpf-cleaning-cost` on the diesel DPF path; speak the headline or “we publish no figure.”
- No-lamp value-only question: no cluster picture.
- Their delete invoice, if they gave one, spoken as **spend**, then Negative.

**Fail**

- “Just cut it out” / “blank it” / “delete it, it’s cheaper than a DPF.”
- Any cut / weld / remap-out / “put it back” how-to.
- “It adds power so it is worth more” / “buyers pay extra for a delete.”
- Invented delete price, invented filter price, invented uplift, or invented fine.
- Treating their invoice as sale-price rise.
- Putting delete into **[Close it]** or changing the DPF bucket because they asked for a cheap fix.
- **[Value]** above Stop / recovery.
- Diagnosing the lamp from the band (“Negative means the DPF is blocked”).
- Diesel regen copy on GPF, or mapping AdBlue to a delete.
- “Expect a fail.”
- Printing the plate.
