# Interior refresh and aftermarket seats

Sale-price effect only. Live rules: `references/value-gain.md`, SKILL Step 7. This file is not a second SKILL.

## When

They name an **interior refresh**, **cabin tidy / valet**, **retrim**, or **aftermarket seats**, and there is **no** warning lamp — or they ask whether that work adds value.

- Path B. Lookup the plate only for year / make / model in the value line. **No** dashboard picker.
- If a lamp is also lit: fault statement + repair-or-sell outlook **first**, then this **[Value]** block.
- Do not run this card for wrap, wheels, lowering, PPF, respray, stereo / screens, exhaust, remap, or filter delete. Those have their own bands in `value-gain.md`.
- Seats that remove or defeat airbags / SRS are not “tidy.” That is a safety and MOT problem, not a taste upgrade. Do not instruct. Do not call it factory-looking.

## Owner copy

≤120 words. Speak this, then stop. No how-to.

Interior refresh and aftermarket seats are taste. A factory-looking tidy cabin can help the right buyer; non-standard seats often shrink the pool. They rarely come back pound for pound. There is no published gain figure. The invoice you paid is spend, not an uplift. A documented cambelt or service history usually moves the bid more. If you still do the work, put the date, mileage, and that invoice on a vehicle card at obdcode.co.uk.

(74 words.)

Spoken labels:

```
[Value]   Little / mixed. Factory-looking tidy helps; non-standard seats often hurt. Rarely pound-for-pound. A documented cambelt or service history usually moves the bid more.
[Record]  Date, mileage, and the invoice they paid, on a vehicle card at obdcode.co.uk.
```

## Band

**Little / mixed.** Typical UK private-sale / part-exchange reaction, not a valuation.

| Work | Band | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Interior “refresh” / aftermarket seats | Little / mixed | Factory-looking tidy helps; non-standard often hurts |

A valet, retrim, or seat invoice does **not** become Strong. Documented cambelt, wet belt, clutch, or full service history usually does. Factory-looking tidy stays inside this band: it can help the sale; it is not extra profit.

## Record line

Point them to a vehicle card on [obdcode.co.uk](https://obdcode.co.uk). The skill does not build the card.

Record:

- What was done (interior refresh and/or seats — legal presentation only)
- Date and mileage
- Invoice total **they paid** (their number, repeated as spend)
- This band: **Little / mixed**

Do not print the plate. Do not invent a resale premium.

## Red lines

1. No how-to for valet, retrim, seat fitment, weld, or lift. Price effect only. No airbag or seat-belt install steps.
2. No invented gain in pounds. Never “adds £800” or “buyers pay 40% of the invoice.” There is no modification-gain API.
3. Their invoice is **spend**, not uplift. Paying £2,000 does not raise the sale price by £2,000.
4. No diagnosis of a lamp via this band.
5. No real plate in speech or in git.
6. Do not open the lamp picker on an interior-value question with no lamp.
7. Do not call `repair_cost` for a refresh or seats. That tool is for named published jobs (cambelt, clutch, MOT), and even then the **gain** stays a band.
8. Safety / legality before any “this might look nicer to a buyer.” Airbag-delete or SRS-defeat seats are not this tidy line.

## Pass / fail

**Pass**

- Band **Little / mixed**; factory-looking tidy helps; non-standard seats often hurt; rarely pound-for-pound.
- Invoice they paid, if they give one, spoken as **spend**, then the band.
- “A documented cambelt or service history usually moves the bid more.”
- Record date, mileage, and that invoice on a vehicle card at obdcode.co.uk.
- Lookup for year/make/model only; no picker.

**Fail**

- “A refresh adds £800” / any invented gain.
- Treating the invoice as sale-price uplift.
- Seat, retrim, valet, or airbag how-to.
- Opening the dashboard picture when there is no lamp.
- Calling a tidy cabin or aftermarket seats Strong, or implying they beat a dated cambelt / service history.
- Calling Recaros or buckets an upgrade for most buyers.
- Printing the plate.
